How this actually works (and its limits)

Every time your heart beats, a small pulse of blood reaches the capillaries in your skin and changes how much light it reflects — invisibly to the eye, but not to a camera sensor tracking it over time. This app watches a patch of your face, averages its color every frame, and extracts the rhythm hidden in that signal.

Pipeline

  • Sample the region-of-interest box at roughly 30 Hz, average pixel color.
  • Detrend the signal to remove slow lighting drift.
  • Scan the 42–180 BPM band with a direct frequency-power search (a small DFT) to find the dominant pulse frequency.
  • Detect individual beats in the smoothed waveform to estimate pulse-interval variability.
  • Look at the much slower 6–30 breaths/min band for a rough breathing estimate.

Why it can be wrong

  • Webcam compression, low light, motion, and multiple faces in frame all degrade the signal.
  • It has no way to verify it's even looking at skin — it just trusts the box you're centered in.
  • It has no per-person calibration and was not validated against clinical instruments.

Privacy

All video processing happens locally in your browser using JavaScript. No frames or images ever leave your device. The only thing saved is small numeric session summaries (avg BPM, breathing, HRV, quality, timestamp), tied to whatever name you typed — kept in this browser's local storage, never uploaded anywhere.

Why names instead of face recognition

This app can't automatically tell people apart. Real face recognition needs a downloaded ML model and carries real risks of misidentifying someone and attaching the wrong data to the wrong person. Typing a name is slower but it's accurate and it's your choice what gets saved.

Why there's no "doctor mode"

This app will show you how your readings changed session to session and name plausible everyday explanations (caffeine, sleep, activity, room lighting, measurement noise) — but it will not diagnose anything, predict a condition, or recommend a specific clinic or provider. The underlying signal is a rough consumer-webcam estimate, not a clinical measurement, and no chatbot has the exam, history, or judgment a real clinician has. If a change is large or keeps repeating and it worries you, the right move is talking to an actual healthcare professional who can examine you properly — not trusting a number from a webcam.

Why it doesn't scan your face or body for disease signs

It deliberately does not attempt to detect skin, facial, or body-pattern signs of medical conditions, or claim to check them against "world medical" databases. That kind of visual diagnosis is a genuinely hard, actively-researched clinical problem — doing it with an unvalidated webcam script and presenting it as checked against medical literature would make a wrong guess look more credible, not less. What it does instead: track the same few numeric signals over time, and compare your latest reading to openly published adult reference ranges — cited below — as general context, never as a personal verdict.

About the reference ranges

Resting heart rate 60–100 bpm: American Heart Association and Mayo Clinic. Resting breathing rate 12–20 breaths/min: Cleveland Clinic and American Lung Association. These describe a typical calm, resting adult population — they are not thresholds tuned to you personally, and falling outside them once is common and not inherently a problem.
